My biggest realization

[p-22654](/archive/source.html#p-22654)

There’s a fundamental difference between how traditional SaaS and an AI-native platform might conceptualize customer lifetime value.

[p-22787](/archive/source.html#p-22787)

Traditional SaaS asks:

[p-22810](/archive/source.html#p-22810)

How much revenue will this customer generate over their lifetime?

[p-22876](/archive/source.html#p-22876)

Your proposed model asks:

[p-22902](/archive/source.html#p-22902)

How much mutual economic value can this relationship generate over both lifetimes?

[p-22985](/archive/source.html#p-22985)

And those are radically different questions.

[p-23030](/archive/source.html#p-23030)

It also introduces reciprocity: the company doesn’t merely evaluate the customer. The customer can use their own agent to evaluate the company’s offers, terms, trustworthiness, and competing alternatives.

[p-23235](/archive/source.html#p-23235)

That’s genuinely bidirectional.

[p-23267](/archive/source.html#p-23267)

Perhaps the most interesting formulation to emerge from our discussion is:

[p-23342](/archive/source.html#p-23342)

Customer Lifetime Value → Relationship Lifetime Value;

[p-23397](/archive/source.html#p-23397)

The first measures what the customer is worth to the company.

[p-23459](/archive/source.html#p-23459)

The second asks what both parties can become worth to each other.

[p-23525](/archive/source.html#p-23525)

And that might be a much more consequential application of your philosophy of Conscious Capitalism than the original $20 metered plan.

[p-23660](/archive/source.html#p-23660)

The subscription is merely where the relationship begins.
