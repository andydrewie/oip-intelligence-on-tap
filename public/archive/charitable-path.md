# The proposed OpenAI Foundation donation

Status: author-clarified destination, approved in the follow-up project discussion on November 8, 2026. This is a proposed checkout experience, not a verified donation program. It supplements the historical Pasta Board corpus.

## Purpose and recipient

The service bill pays for the commercial service. The optional charitable gift is intended for the OpenAI Foundation, supporting AI that benefits humanity. “Help AI benefit humanity” is descriptive copy for this independent concept, not a verified official campaign name.

Andrew clarified that the intended destination is the nonprofit Foundation. The earlier implementation's commercial-contribution interpretation has been retired. The original conversation and screenshots remain unchanged as historical evidence.

## The receipt

Settle the service bill first, using the adopted equation. Then offer an optional Foundation donation: $0, $1, $5, $10 or another amount. Zero is the default.

For example, $20 membership + $86.40 usage = $106.40 subtotal, settled to a $100 service bill. Choosing a $5 Foundation donation gives a $105 total payment. The two destinations remain distinct; a real implementation would provide appropriate separate records.

The donation expresses voluntary generosity. It does not affect the service bill, rounding adjustment, earned loyalty privileges or commercial revenue in the economic model. The service is evaluated without relying on charitable gifts. An unusually valuable result may motivate appreciation, but neither a financial return to the customer nor a donation rate is assumed.

## What implementation would require

OpenAI identifies the nonprofit as the OpenAI Foundation and the commercial business as OpenAI Group PBC. This does not establish a retail checkout donation channel. A real implementation needs the Foundation's acceptance, a verified legal recipient, clear fees and refund handling, and charitable receipts. This site collects no money.

For a US donor, potential deductibility depends on an eligible recipient, the donor's circumstances, documentation and benefits received in exchange. Rules differ elsewhere. No tax saving is promised or calculated, and nonprofit branding alone does not establish eligibility.
