# Research agenda

**Test whether the design helps people complete useful work within a budget they understand and choose.** The prototype provides reproducible mechanics and explicit assumptions. It does not yet provide customer experiments or evidence of wider access.

## Falsifiable questions

| Hypothesis | Evidence that would support it | Evidence that would challenge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The receipt improves understanding | Better prediction of settled bills and recovery from mistakes | Confusion persists, especially near boundaries |
| Optional metering broadens useful access | More completed tasks within chosen budgets at matched quality | More spending anxiety, surprise bills or abandonment |
| Loyalty creates mutual value | Cohort evidence of useful outcomes and sustainable retention | Rewards mainly subsidize usage that would occur anyway |
| Relationship support helps pioneers | Better outcomes than a suitable comparison with measured support costs | Selection effects explain the apparent improvement |
| The ecosystem benefits newcomers | Meaningful participation and progress beyond established high spenders | Benefits concentrate while the entry path becomes less useful |

## Separate three kinds of evidence

**Engineering evidence** checks arithmetic, validation, state transitions, persistence and reproducibility. The repository's automated tests speak to these questions.

**Human evidence** checks comprehension, accessibility, task success and experienced uncertainty. A responsive layout or a passed regression test does not establish these outcomes.

**Economic evidence** compares delivered value, resources, capacity and behavior. A successful participant story does not by itself establish causality or viable unit economics.

## A bounded first study

1. Choose one task domain, a clearly described participant population and a relevant alternative, including existing fixed-plan and credit experiences.
2. Begin with synthetic bills. Include both downward adjustments and sharp upward boundaries, interrupted input and recovery.
3. Define task quality, time horizon, budget comprehension and useful completion before seeing results. Report uncertainty and missing data.
4. Evaluate newcomers, low-spend users and established users separately. Keep the proposal's intended recognition of spend, tenure and reliability visible.
5. Only consider a paid pilot after defining the actual meter, spending controls, costs and participant terms.

For the wider ecosystem, evaluate mentorship, research support and founder support separately. Record unsuccessful projects alongside successes. Attribute outcomes cautiously; people who volunteer for a program may already differ from those who do not.

## What to contribute now

A counterexample to a claim, a better comparator, a proposed study design or a reproducible defect is welcome. Use the repository's [claim challenge or bug forms](https://github.com/andydrewie/oip-intelligence-on-tap/issues/new/choose). State what observation would change your mind.

Do not submit private account information, confidential research or sensitive personal data as public evidence.
